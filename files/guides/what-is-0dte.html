What is 0DTE (and 1DTE)?

A plain definition of zero-days-to-expiration and next-session options — why the labels matter for SPY/SPX market structure, without turning definitions into trade instructions.

Educational and informational only. Not investment advice.

Definition Why people search it What changes as expiration nears SPY and SPX context Conditions vs calls

01

Definition

0DTE means an option that expires on the same trading day. 1DTE means it expires on the next trading session. The labels are calendars, not strategies.

0DTE

  • Expires today
  • Often used in conversations about same-day SPY/SPX option activity
  • Leaves little time for “wait and see” once a session is underway

1DTE

  • Expires next session
  • Still short-dated, but not the same clock as same-day expiration
  • Useful when comparing overnight vs intraday structure

02

Why people search for 0DTE

Search interest clusters around short-dated index products because activity, attention, and educational questions concentrate there. Common intents include:

  • What the acronym means
  • How SPY 0DTE differs from SPX 0DTE
  • How same-day expiration changes the feel of market moves
  • How services describe conditions around short-dated options

None of those intents require a recommendation. Clear definitions reduce confusion before anyone discusses tools or publications.

03

What changes as expiration nears

Time is scarce

With little time left to expiration, prices can move quickly relative to longer-dated options. That is a mechanical feature of short-dated contracts — not a reason to trade them.

Attention clusters

Intraday commentary often focuses on near-term expirations because that is where a large share of listed activity sits. Educational material should name the clock it is talking about.

Language gets sloppy

“0DTE signal” can mean anything from a chat tip to a rules-based observation. Ask whether the source is describing a condition, recommending a trade, or selling a narrative after the fact.

04

SPY and SPX context

Most 0DTE education in U.S. index options points at SPY (ETF) and SPX (index). They reference the same equity-index complex but differ in packaging, size, and settlement. See SPY vs SPX for 0DTE for the comparison.

05

Conditions vs calls

Knowing what 0DTE means does not tell you what to buy or sell. A market-condition publication may note observed structure in SPY/SPX short-dated context; a trade alert tells you what to do next. Those are different products.
