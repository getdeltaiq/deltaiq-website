What “bull” and “bear” mean in market-condition publications

In everyday talk, bull and bear suggest direction. In a rules-based publication, they are labels for a defined observational state at publication time — not forecasts and not trade instructions.

Educational and informational only. Not investment advice.

Everyday meaning Snapshot Publication contents Language Shared labels

01

Everyday meaning vs publication labels

Casually, “bullish” or “bearish” can mean a personal opinion about future price. In DeltaIQ’s glossary, labels such as Currently Bull or Currently Bear mark a rules-evaluated condition state at the moment of publication.

02

Snapshot, not forecast

A condition label is a snapshot under a fixed process. It can change when inputs change. It is not a prediction, target, or guarantee.

See the Publication glossary for the three-line dashboard format.

03

What a three-line publication includes / excludes

  • Often includes: a condition label, live reference-price context, and disclaimer framing
  • Does not include: personalized strikes, position size, entry/exit timing, or stop instructions

See Market conditions vs trade alerts.

04

Observational vs instructional language

Observational

“Defined conditions are present at publication time.” Describes a state.

Instructional

“Buy this strike, this size, now.” Directs an account action.

05

Why every subscriber sees the same label

A shared, simultaneous publication is a design choice: the same rules, the same output, the same second — not personalized advice. See Methodology and Rules-based vs discretionary signals.
