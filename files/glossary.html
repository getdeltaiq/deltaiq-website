Alert Glossary

A simple guide to understanding your DeltaIQ text alerts.

Alerts describe observed conditions and do not guarantee results. Informational only — not personalized investment advice.

What’s in an alert Alert types Reading the numbers Reminders

01

What’s in a text alert

Every alert you get is built the same basic way:

  • A name for the alert (like “Strong Entrance” or “Runway Qualified”)
  • A direction label — BULL or BEAR — used by the publication for the observed condition set (not a prediction or instruction)
  • A reference price level shown in the alert (observational; not an instruction to buy or sell)
  • A quick historical summary — how similar published alerts have looked relative to later market movement (illustrative only)
  • A current price snapshot of SPY
  • A clearer / more cautious mark — how the model’s rules classified conditions at send time
  • A typical timeframe from past examples — how long similar observed moves have taken historically (not a guarantee)

02

Types of alerts

Here’s what each alert name means:

Strong Entrance

Conditions appear aligned under the model’s rules; the most common type of condition alert.

High Potential Entry

Historical patterns associated with earlier publication timing than a Strong Entrance — less confirmed by design.

Early Entrance

Historical patterns associated with very early publication timing — less confirmed than High Potential Entry by design.

Runway Qualified

A Strong Entrance condition set where additional rules also fired (extra checks on the same automated cycle).

Late Fade

Conditions under the model’s rules associated historically with a change in the then-prevailing direction.

Macro Impulse

A sudden, sharp observed price move, often near news or economic releases; historically short windows.

Trend Continuation

An existing directional move paused; conditions associated historically with the move resuming.

Trend Join

Conditions published after a move is already underway (early portion may have been missed).

Lower Band Bear

A downward (bear) condition set with additional supporting signs under the model’s rules.

Pin Session

A calmer pattern where price has historically tended to stay near its average rather than trend strongly.

03

Reading the numbers on your alert

What you see
What it means
Reference Level (may appear as “Entry Point” in the text)
A price level shown for context with the observed conditions — not an instruction to enter a trade.
Track Record / History
A quick illustrative summary of how similar published alerts related to later market movement in past examples. Not a guarantee.
Live Price
SPY price at the moment the alert was sent.
Confidence Reading
How the model’s rules scored condition alignment at send time. Higher means more rules aligned — not a recommendation.
Clearer / Caution
Whether the automated rules marked conditions as clearer or more cautious at send time.
Typical Hold Time
How long similar observed moves have typically taken in past examples. A historical guide, not a guarantee.

04

A couple of reminders

  • BULL / BEAR are publication labels for the observed condition set — not predictions or trade instructions.
  • All of the numbers you see are based on historical patterns, not guarantees of what will happen next.
  • These alerts are informational and not personalized financial advice. You alone decide what, if anything, to do.
